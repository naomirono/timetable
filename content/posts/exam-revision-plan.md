---
title: "How to plan exam revision without cramming"
description: "Work backwards from your exam dates to build a realistic revision plan, with spaced practice and time to rest."
date: "2026-11-05"
---

Cramming feels productive, but it fades fast. A plan that starts early and works backwards from each exam date is calmer and more effective.

## Put every exam in one list

Write down the **date, time and venue** of each exam. Seeing them together shows you where the busy weeks are, so you can start earlier for the clusters.

## Work backwards

For each exam, count the weeks you have. Then split the syllabus into topics and spread them across those weeks, leaving the final week for past papers and gaps.

## Use short, repeated sessions

Revisit each topic several times over a few weeks instead of once for hours. This is called **spaced practice**, and it is one of the best-supported study methods there is.

## Practise recalling, not rereading

Close your notes and write down what you remember. Do past papers under timed conditions. Rereading feels easy because the material looks familiar, but it teaches you less.

## Build in rest

Plan one lighter day each week and keep your sleep steady. Tired revision is slow revision.

## Set reminders early

Ask for a reminder about a week before each exam, so starting revision is a decision you made in advance, not a panic. Class Timetable can remind you a week ahead and keeps each exam's date, time and venue together.
