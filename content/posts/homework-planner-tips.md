Missed deadlines are rarely about ability. They usually come from tasks that were never written down, or written down with no date. These habits fix most of it.

## Write it down the moment you hear it

Add the task before you leave the classroom. Include the **due date** and a one-line note about what is needed, such as the page numbers or the word count.

## Sort by course

Grouping tasks by course makes it easy to see what each teacher expects this week. It also stops one busy subject hiding the others.

## Check three lists every day

Look at what is **overdue**, what is **due today** and what is **coming up**. Clear overdue work first, because it only gets harder the longer it waits.

## Break big tasks into smaller ones

"Write essay" is vague. "Plan essay", "write introduction" and "edit" are tasks you can finish in a sitting. Give each its own date before the real deadline, and slot them into the gaps in your [weekly timetable](/blog/how-to-make-a-class-timetable).

## Keep links with the task

If the task depends on a web page, a shared document or an online portal, save the link with it. You will not have to search for it at 11pm.

## Let your phone do the nagging

A reminder before the deadline beats good intentions. In Class Timetable you can add homework with due dates, notes and links, and set custom reminders for each task. When exams approach, switch to the [exam revision plan](/blog/exam-revision-plan).
